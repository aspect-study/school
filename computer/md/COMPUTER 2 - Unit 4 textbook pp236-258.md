## Let's Browse Back — Modifying the Quick Launch Bar and the Notification Area

The **Quick Launch bar** provides a quick and easy way to start a program.

To add an icon on the Quick Launch bar, we:
1. Drag any shortcut, file, folder, or drive over the Quick Launch bar on the taskbar.
2. Drop the item to create its shortcut on the Quick Launch bar.

Another way is by right-clicking the shortcut on the desktop, then choosing **Pin to taskbar.**

To remove an icon from the Quick Launch bar, we:
1. Right-click it to delete it from the Quick Launch bar.
2. Choose **Unpin from taskbar.**

To change the date and time settings, we:
1. Right-click the date and time on the Notification area.
2. Choose **Adjust date/time** option.
3. Choose **Date & time.**
4. The **Set time automatically** should be turned off.
5. Select **Change** button to bring the changes into effect.

## Lesson 1 — Understanding the Internet (pp. 236–240)

Let's See — At the end of the lesson, we should be able to:
1. Describe the Internet.
2. Explain that information is exchanged through the Internet.
3. Recognize the history of the Internet and its current trend.

Let's Ask Ourselves: How does the Internet work? How did the Internet begin? What is happening now on the Internet?

Let's Discover: Think of a time when your family talked with a relative using the Internet through a chat, or voice or video call. Surely, they have exchanged greetings, shared stories, and had a good laugh!

**Connections with the Internet.** Ever wondered how the computers made it happen?
A message of greetings was transferred from a computer all the way to a relative's computer. Computers are connected. We can still communicate even if we are not in the same place.
What is more fascinating is that another relative using their computer can also join the conversation.

All of these are possible because of the Internet. The **Internet** is the physical connection of computers with one another.

Internet came from the words **interconnected computer networks**. A computer is connected to a network of computers, and its network is connected to other networks. Imagine how many computers are connected to the Internet!

When computers are connected, there is an exchange of information. Greetings and stories are examples of it. Using the computer, anyone can process, store, or share information. The Internet has a large amount of it shared by different people.

Let us take a look at the growth of the Internet.

**Internet – Then and Now.** The Internet was invented to share and keep information safe and stable. Way back in the 1960s, the early networks were only used in the government, research, and military. Eventually, these networks were opened to universities. In the early 1990s, the Internet became accessible to everyone.

Today, connecting to the Internet has become easier and cheaper. Common things and machines can now be connected to the Internet.

This is called the **Internet of Things** or **IoT**. It includes smartphones, watches, parts of a car, and many more. We can do a lot of things using a smartphone. We can call, send messages, use apps, and surf for information.

(Speech bubble) Give more examples of devices or machines that can connect to the Internet.

The Internet has developed from a small network to a bigger network. Imagine how the Internet will be like in the future.

## Lesson 2 — Exploring the Places on the Internet (pp. 245–248)

Let's See — At the end of the lesson, we should be able to:
1. Identify the uses of the Internet.
2. Recognize good websites.

Let's Ask Ourselves: What activities can be done on the Internet? How do we identify a good website?

Let's Discover: Have you been to places? Think of a place and describe it. Who was with you when you went there? What did you do in that place?

**Places We Can Go.** The playground is one of the places that usually come into mind. We may have gone there with an adult before. It is a fun place where we can play all around using swings, seesaws, slides, and meet other children.

**Places on the Internet.** **Websites** are the different places on the Internet where we can do different activities such as:
1. Watching videos
2. Listening to music
3. Playing online games
4. Searching for hobbies and interests
5. Visiting school websites
6. Sending e-mails to family and friends
7. Researching for projects

**Places Where We Went.** After visiting places, there are those that we like and dislike. Why do we find some websites enjoyable while others not?
- You were with people when you went there. Both of you may like or dislike those places.
- How long did you stay in those places? Perhaps you stayed for a few hours or days. It depends on your parents or the adults that you were with.

Same goes when visiting places on the Internet. There are websites that some people may find enjoyable and for some not as much.

**Enjoyable Websites.** Here are some questions to think about when deciding if a website is enjoyable:
1. Is it for our age?
2. What is the topic?
3. Do we like the topic?
4. Can we find the author's name?
5. Do we like the pictures and videos?
6. When we click to another page, can we go back?
7. Did we have fun visiting the site?
8. How long did we visit the site?

## Lesson 3 — Connecting to the Internet (pp. 253–258)

Let's See — At the end of the lesson, we should be able to recognize how computers are connected to the Internet.

Let's Ask Ourselves: How are computers connected to the Internet?

Let's Discover: Look around. Are there people using the Internet? What devices are they using?
In the previous lesson, we have learned the activities that we can do on the Internet. Those activities are fun; at the same time, they also allow us to learn something new and exciting.

**Internet Devices.** Good thing, we can connect to the Internet using any of these devices: Desktop computer, Tablet, Laptop computer, Smartphone. Let us find out how we connect them to the Internet.

**Internet Connection.** The homeowners need to have an Internet connection. They called an **Internet Service Provider (ISP)**. This is a company that can connect anyone to the Internet.

The ISP told the homeowners that they can connect through dial-up, Digital Subscriber Line (DSL), cable, 3G, or 4G.
- **Dial-up** uses a phone line and it is usually the slowest.
- **DSL** and **cable** are both broadband connections and offer better Internet service. DSL uses a phone line but is faster than dial-up. Cable uses a cable TV connection.
- **3G (third generation)** and **4G (fourth generation)** are wireless connections. These are often used on mobile devices. It can connect to the Internet anywhere and anytime.

The homeowners chose DSL. An ISP technician went to their house and brought a device. Guess what it is. (Picture: Modem)

The ISP technician brought a **modem**. The modem is a device used when connecting to the Internet.
The ISP technician connected the cables of the modem to the phone line and to the computer. This way, signals coming from each end will be interpreted by the modem. The phone and the computer can now understand the signals from each other. (Picture: Phone line ↔ Modem ↔ Desktop computer)

To check if there is a connection, the ISP technician opened a web browser. Now they can use the Internet.

The homeowners also wanted to connect to the Internet using their cell phones and tablets. As a result, they used a **wireless router** for wireless connections. This is known as **Wireless Fidelity** or **Wi-Fi**. (Picture: Router → Smartphone, Tablet — the router has an antenna)

This is how we can connect to the Internet: **from the computer at home, the connection passes to the modem, then to the phone line. It then connects to the ISP, and then to the Internet.**
(Diagram: house with Computer and Modem → Phone line → Internet Service Provider (ISP) → Internet)

Remember to always ask for permission and assistance from an adult to stay safe when connecting the computer to the Internet.
