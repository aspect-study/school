 Discussion

A group of words that does not express
complete thought is called a nonsentence.
It is also called a fragment.

 Examples:
 the book
 played the sand
 RULES TO MAKE SENTENCES CLEAR AND
 CORRECT WHEN WRITING

 1.Begin the sentence with a capital letter.
 2.End the sentence with the correct

 punctuation mark.

Punctuation mark ­ is a symbol that lets us
understand sentences better. A sentence
may end with a period (.), a question mark (?),
or an exclamation mark (!).

 3.Spell the words correctly.
 SUBJECT AND PREDICATE

A complete sentence has two important parts.
 1.Subject ­ is who or what the sentence is
 about
 2.Predicate ­ is the part that tells something
 about the subject
 SUBJECT AND PREDICATE

Examples:
 Claudia wants to be a flight attendant.

subject predicate

 Theodore is a good speaker.

 subject predicate

 Warm-up!

 "Ou Ou Wants to Have Friends"

Ou Ou, the little porcupine likes to dance, sing, and
play. What he does not like are the sharp spines all
over his body called quills. They get in the way of
his fun! No one else likes them either. At the
playground, no one wants to play with him. At camp,
no one wants to share a tent with him.
"Don't sleep with us, Ou Ou," they complain. "Your
quills will poke us."
 Warm-up!

 "Ou Ou Wants to Have Friends"

One day, a little mouse approaches and says,
"Ou, Ou, your quills make you special." The little mouse
wants to help, so he makes a special shirt for Ou Ou to
wear.
Ou Ou wears the shirt and goes out to meet the other
animals.
"Don't come near us! We are afraid of the sharp quills
on your back," they say.
"What quills?" asks Ou Ou. "I do not have any."
 Warm-up!

 "Ou Ou Wants to Have Friends"

The other animals laugh. Ou Ou sees that his quills
have pierced through his special new shirt. Feeling sad,
Ou Ou goes back to his only friend, the little mouse.
"Do not worry my prickly friend," the little mouse says.
"I have another idea."
"What are you doing?" Ou Ou asks.
"Just wait and see, Ou Ou," says the little mouse.
"Now, let us go and meet the other animals," the little
mouse suggests.
 Warm-up!

 "Ou Ou Wants to Have Friends"

Ou Ou is excited to show off his new quills.
Everyone comes over to admire Ou Ou's colorful new
quills, and soon they realize they are not so scary
after all.
"We are sorry, Ou Ou," they say. They all go play
together.
 Warm-up!

 Guide questions:

1. Who are the characters in the story?
2. What first happened in the story.
3. What did the little mouse gave to Ou?
4. Did the other animals likes Ou's colorful quills?
 Discussion

Sequence
means the order in which events happen.

Events are what happened at the start, in
the middle, and at the end of a story.
 Discussion

Beginning What was the problem of the main character?

Middle How did the little mouse help him?

End What did Ou Ou understand at the end of
 the story?
 Discussion

When telling or retelling a story, you
can use signal words to clearly tell which
happened at the beginning, middle, and
end.
The most common are "first," "next,"
"then," and "last."
 Discussion

Example:

First, Ou Ou wanted to make friends. But he
could not do it because the other animals
were afraid of his quills. Then, Ou Ou met
the little mouse. The two became friends.
Next, the little mouse helped Ou Ou solved
his quill problem. Last, Ou Ou finally made
friends with the other animals.
 Discussion

REMEMBER

 Sequencing means arranging the events in
 order.
 Signal words are used to show sequence of
 events.
